# Awesome Weight-Space Programming [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

[![Check](https://github.com/eladb3/awesome-weight-space-programming/actions/workflows/check.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/eladb3/awesome-weight-space-programming/actions/workflows/check.yml)

> A curated list of papers on reading, editing, and writing the weights of neural networks directly.

**Weight-space programming (WSP)** is the practice of having a language model or agent work on a neural network's weights directly: *read* them to say what the network does, *edit* them to fix a failure or change a behaviour, or *write* them from scratch from a specification. It is guided by a goal stated in natural language instead of by gradient descent on data. The term was introduced by Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab in ["Sparks of Weight-Space Programming"](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) (MIT CSAIL, November 2026). Within weeks it was followed by replications, critiques, benchmarks, security studies, and applications from robotics to medicine.

The list has three parts:

- **[Weight-Space Programming](#weight-space-programming)**: the papers on LLMs and agents reading, editing, and writing weights.
- **[Foundations and Related Areas](#foundations-and-related-areas)**: older lines of work that already treat weights as something to compute, compose, learn from, or read. These are model and knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT, MEND and successors), task arithmetic and model merging, weight-space learning, weight generation (hypernetworks, diffusion over weights, text-to-LoRA), and weight-based interpretability.
- **[Resources](#resources)**: benchmarks, datasets, tools, model zoos, and surveys.

Out of scope: ordinary fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning that is only a training recipe, activation steering with no weight change, and pruning or quantization unless the paper is about weight-space structure.

Entries are sorted newest first within each section. Every entry has been checked against its arXiv or publisher page.

Publication dates are those of the first public version, usually arXiv v1. Citation counts are from [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/), updated 2026-11-25.

## Weight-Space Programming

Language models and agents that read, edit, or write the weights of other networks (or their own) directly, guided by a goal in natural language rather than by gradient descent.

### Reading Weights

Models that inspect another network's weights or weight diffs and say what the network does.

- [From LoRA to Language: Recovering Skill Documents from Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09454) - *Saranya Dintakurthy Swadha et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-06, 0 citations.* Tries to recover the skill document that a LoRA adapter was compiled from, comparing weights-only readers with agents that run or diff the adapter.
- [Weight-space programming for auditing clinical decision networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05341) - *Humza Nusrat and Omar Nusrat, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-03, 5 citations.* An LLM audits clinical decision networks from their weights alone and repairs a hidden shortcut by changing a handful of parameters. [[Code](https://github.com/hnusrat1/weightspace-rt)]
- [Weight Oracles: Reading Neural Network Weights with Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07334) - *Krishna Kabra et al., NeurIPS 2026 Workshop. Published 2026-10-05, 11 citations.* Fine-tunes LLMs to read another network's raw weights as tokens and answer diagnostic questions about it, such as whether it contains a backdoor.
- [Imprint Reader: From Weight-Update Readout to Behavioral Intervention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35261) - *Guanxu Chen et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-09-28, 0 citations.* Trains a reader model that takes a frozen weight update as input and describes in words what it taught, then reuses the reader's gradients to select and prune weight-update coordinates toward a stated behavior.
- [Learning to Interpret Weight Differences in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05092) - *Avichal Goel et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2025-10-06, 22 citations.* Trains a model to describe its own fine-tuning weight diff in natural language, exposing hidden behaviours and new knowledge from weights alone. [[Code](https://github.com/Aviously/diff-interpretation-tuning)]

### Editing and Repairing Models

An LLM or agent changes an existing network's weights to fix a failure or change a behaviour.

- [TowerBridge: Bridging the Dual-Encoder Modality Gap via Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31233) - *Hani Alomari et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-23, 0 citations.* An LLM agent inspects both CLIP towers and writes closed-form weight edits that fix hard-negative retrieval, and its recurring edit is then distilled into a reusable recipe.
- [Weight Coding: Evolving Web-Scale Recommenders through Direct Weight Edits with LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29613) - *Junpeng Hou et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-22, 0 citations.* Brings agent-driven weight editing to production-scale recommenders, where an LLM agent hypothesises and executes direct parameter edits that beat a post-training fine-tune.
- [Writing Weights or Training Them? Spatial Reasoning through Weight-Space Programming and Low-Rank Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27089) - *Yalin Yang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-20, 0 citations.* Compares an LLM-written weight patch with LoRA fine-tuning on spatial-reasoning tasks for a small LM.
- [Weight-space programming and mechanistic interpretability for model editing in particle physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27079) - *Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-20, 0 citations.* Combines LLM weight-space programming with SAE-guided analysis to remove an infrared-safety violation from a particle-physics foundation model by direct weight edits.
- [Weight-Space Programming Writes In-Context Learning and Matches Fine-Tuning in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26841) - *Konstantinos Skianis and Aristidis Likas, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-19, 0 citations.* Frontier editors turn in-context examples into MLP weight edits so a weaker LM performs the task with an empty prompt, matching ICL and LoRA fine-tuning.
- [Training Vision Models for LLM-Driven Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26408) - *Timo Ropinski and Sebastian Kranz, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-19, 1 citation.* Benchmarks LLM weight editing of CNNs and ViTs at scale and proposes training networks to be easier for LLMs to edit.
- [Keel: A Frontier Model Teaches a Vision-Language Model by Rewriting Its Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25257) - *Jiajun Fan et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-18, 0 citations.* A frontier model diagnoses why a small VLM falls into reasoning loops and fixes it by rewriting the VLM's weights, given only the symptom.
- [Can Weight-Space Programming Alter Learned Ethical Priorities?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24330) - *Kai Yi Ji, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-18, 0 citations.* Edits the fitted choice weights of a concept-supervised text model to change how it trades off keeping a pledge against helping more people.
- [In Situ Training of Optical Neural Networks by Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23875) - *Ilker Oguz et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-18, 0 citations.* Extends weight-space programming to physical hardware: a coding agent tunes an optical neural network from camera measurements, without a calibrated digital twin or gradients.
- [HypoLoop: Revising Weight-Editing Plans from Scientific Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23718) - *Mengran Li et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-18, 0 citations.* Lets LLM-guided policies choose which weights to edit in scientific predictors and revise that editing plan from experimental feedback, with solvers computing the patches. [[Code](https://github.com/limengran98/HypoLoop)]
- [Weight Space Programming for Vision Language Model Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23615) - *Avinash Goutham Aluguvelly, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-17, 0 citations.* Plants faults in VLM weights and checks each repair a coding agent makes, revealing which cues gradient-free weight repairs rely on.
- [What Does Weight-Space Programming Add to Diffusion Editing? Program Construction versus Score-Guided Refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22228) - *Zitian Gao et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-17, 0 citations.* Compares LM-written weight-update programs for diffusion concept editing against published editors and finds a mostly negative result.
- [Can AI Mature a Model's Geometry? Training-Free Weight Baking in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21310) - *Amin Golnari and Saeid Sanei, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 0 citations.* An analyst model reads topological summaries of a frozen LM's layers and proposes a transformation that is written into its FFN weights in closed form.
- [Weight-Space Programming Changes How an LLM Uses Python for Computational Biology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20566) - *Makoto Sato, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 0 citations.* A frontier LLM edits a small LM's weights and changes how it uses Python on computational-biology problems, nearly doubling strictly correct answers.
- [Weights & Caches: LLMs Can Program Tabular Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20174) - *Shi Bin Hoo et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-15, 0 citations.* Shows that LLMs can program statistical computations into a tabular foundation model's weights directly.
- [Mixed Signals: Weight-Space Programming for EEG and ECG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13758) - *Dara Varam et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-10.* Applies LLM weight reading and editing to EEG and ECG models, repairing planted faults by reasoning rather than gradient descent.
- [Exploring Weight-Space Programming: Capabilities, Generalization, and Limitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382) - *Junru Hu et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-06, 12 citations.* Systematic study of agent weight-editing trajectories on small LMs: small localized edits can give large gains, but generalization across tasks is inconsistent.
- [Can LLMs Debug Robots by Editing Their Weights?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09376) - *Jusheng Zhang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-06, 5 citations.* An LLM repairs a robot foundation policy's instruction grounding by editing a few dozen embedding rows with simulator feedback and no gradient updates.
- [WeightLoop: Weight-Space Programming with Experimental Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07319) - *Mengran Li et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-05, 7 citations.* Turns experimental feedback into persistent weight corrections of scientific predictors through LLM-written update programs.
- [Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) - *Rikiya Takehi et al., Blog post (MIT CSAIL). Published 2026-11-02.* The post that introduced weight-space programming, showing that a frontier LLM can read raw weights, repair networks such as a looping small reasoning model, and write small networks from scratch, all without gradients. [[Code](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs)]

### Writing Weights from Scratch

An LLM writes all the weights of a network, or installs a new capability, from a specification instead of training.

- [Looped Weight-Space Programming: LLM-Written Transformers for Long Algorithmic Reasoning](https://mbzuai-oryx.github.io/LoopedWSP/) - *Abdelrahman Elsayed et al., Blog post (MBZUAI). Published 2026-11-23.* An LLM hand-writes every weight of a small looped transformer that repeats one block as long as needed, solving all of the hard mazes it fails to solve itself and scaling to far longer algorithmic tasks. [[Code](https://github.com/mbzuai-oryx/LoopedWSP)]
- [Don't Train It, Write It: An AI Agent Writes New Capabilities into Language Model Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28483) - *Santiago Rivadeneira Quintero, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-21, 0 citations.* An agent installs new capabilities, including exact long multiplication, by adding hand-written gated heads and neurons to a pretrained LLM while leaving its other outputs exactly unchanged.
- [WeightCode: Installing New Capabilities into Neural Networks via Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25476) - *Bo Li et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-19, 1 citation.* A frozen LM writes executable weight programs that install new capabilities into trained vision and navigation networks, outperforming fine-tuning baselines.
- [Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22055) - *Hyunjong Ok and Jaeho Lee, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-17, 0 citations.* Lets LLMs that write weights by hand also choose the architecture, and finds they need not keep the transformer.
- [Weight-Space Programming for Materials Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22013) - *Sk Md Ahnaf Akif Alvi and Raymundo Arróyave, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-17, 0 citations.* An LLM writes the weights of a small candidate-ranking network from a task description, for materials discovery with almost no labels.
- [Weight-space programming for tractor guidance controller: large language models natively write neural network weights that steer a real tractor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21958) - *Timo Oksanen, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 0 citations.* LLMs write and adapt the weights of a 253-parameter steering network that is deployed on a real tractor, with no optimizer.
- [Zero-Shot Weight-Space Programming for Prediction and Classification of Target Variables in Structured Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19292) - *Eduardo C. Garrido-Merchán, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-14, 1 citation.* Has an LLM write every weight of a feed-forward network from a small dataset shown in full, so the LLM must do the statistical inference itself.
- [Interpretable weight-space programming builds transparent transformers and brain encoding models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18114) - *Yash Mehta et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-13, 2 citations.* An LLM agent programs the weights of transparent transformers and brain-encoding models so their computations stay human-readable.
- [Compiling Known Rules into Compact Neural Agents: Weight-Space Programming on Unseen Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16981) - *Jens Lehmann, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-12, 1 citation.* LLM writers compile known game rules directly into the weights of compact recurrent agents that generalize to unseen levels.
- [Weight-Space Programming for Medical Imaging: Do We Still Need Training Data?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12066) - *Maximilian Lindholz et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-09, 1 citation.* Has LLMs write the weights of medical image classifiers from medical knowledge alone, asking whether training data is needed at all.
- [Weight-Space Programming of Policies and Their Verification via Model Checking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11604) - *Dennis Gross, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-08, 1 citation.* Combines LLM-written policy weights with probabilistic model checking so that a written policy can be verified against a formal requirement.
- [Weight-Space Programming of Variational Quantum Circuits: Large Language Models Write Quantum Circuit Parameters Without an Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11537) - *Debarshi Kundu et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-08, 1 citation.* Extends weight writing to quantum computing: an LLM sets variational quantum circuit angles directly, with no optimizer.
- [Physics2Weight: Direct Neural Network Architecture and Weight Generation from Physical Laws Using Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12954) - *Shogo Sato et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-07, 0 citations.* Has an LLM generate both the architecture and the weights of networks that solve PDEs directly from the governing physical laws (Physics2Weight).
- [Can a Language Model Write the Weights? An Empirical Study of LLM-Written Scoring Networks for Column Generation and MILP Branching, with a Bus-Driver Scheduling Application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09559) - *Renan Butkeraites, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-06, 1 citation.* Has an LLM write small scoring networks for column generation and MILP branching in exact optimization solvers.
- [Zero-Shot Weight-Space Programming Across a Difficulty-Graded Ladder of Two-Player Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09271) - *Eduardo C. Garrido-Merchán, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-06, 1 citation.* Tests how far zero-shot hand-writing of network weights goes across games of increasing difficulty, from Nim to a chess endgame.
- [NeuronGen: Neural Network Parameter Generation via Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12470) - *Jiaqi Wang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2025-05-18, 4 citations.* Has an LLM directly emit the parameters of small networks from task, data, and architecture descriptions, an early instance of LLM-written weights.

### Self-Editing Models

Models that produce updates to their own weights.

- [Self-Upgrading Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31117) - *Jiajun Fan et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-23, 0 citations.* A 27B embodied agent reviews its own episode logs and rewrites one of its own weight tensors between tasks, keeping only self-edits that pass its own regression tests.
- [Recursive Self-Editing: LLMs Program Their Own Weight Edits for Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29513) - *Linghua Zhang and Yunhe Li, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-22, 0 citations.* A model writes programs that build low-rank edits to its own weights from white-box primitives, and the edited model writes the next edit, improving over several generations without gradients.
- [Self-WSP: Can Language Models Improve Themselves Through Weight-Space Programming?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28833) - *Guoming Ling et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-21, 0 citations.* Has a small LLM read and edit its own weights and finds large math gains, but also that random-search editing often matches it, questioning whether the model understands its edits.
- [BaseRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement of a Base Vision-Language Model via Training-Free Harness Internalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24578) - *Mingda Zhang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-18, 0 citations.* A vision-language model internalizes the gains of its own prompt-and-tool harness by writing training-free, block-wise edits into its own weights, round after round. [[Code](https://github.com/beita6969/BaseRSI)]
- [Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) - *Jiajun Fan et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 3 citations.* A language model improves itself by reasoning about and rewriting its own weights, with no gradients or training run.
- [Search over Self-Edit Strategies for LLM Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14532) - *Alistair Cheong et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-01-20, 1 citation.* Lets an LLM choose its own self-edit strategy and hyperparameters, deciding from task feedback how its weights should be updated.
- [Self-Adapting Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943) - *Adam Zweiger et al., NeurIPS 2025. Published 2025-06-12, 109 citations.* An LLM generates its own self-edits (fine-tuning data and update directives) that become persistent weight updates, trained with RL (SEAL).

### Replications, Critiques and Alternatives

Independent replications, tests of what the reading and writing really rely on, and non-LLM alternatives to the same edits.

- [Reparameterizing Your Object Detectors for Weight Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29882) - *Chien-Yao Wang, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-23, 0 citations.* A non-LLM route to backprop-free detector editing that lifts layers into function-preserving subspaces where direct weight updates stop stalling, then folds them back. [[Code](https://github.com/WongKinYiu/ODWSP)]
- [When Can a Rewiring Control Test LLM Weight Readers? C2 and an Eligibility Protocol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29120) - *Vaishak Girish Kumar and Sanika Vilas Najan, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-22, 0 citations.* Proposes a readout-permutation counterfactual and an eligibility protocol for telling whether an LLM weight reader computes the network or merely recognizes weight fingerprints.
- [Where Does a Weight-Editing Agent Help? Blind Diagnosis and Repair of Deployment Faults in Vision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28781) - *Zhuangzi Li et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-21, 0 citations.* Tests a weight-editing agent on vision models with known deployment faults and finds a post-hoc script matches it on familiar faults while the agent wins on unfamiliar ones.
- [A CPU Inside an LLM: Zero-Gradient Compilation of Exact Circuits into Pretrained Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26450) - *Tanmay Sah, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-19, 0 citations.* A closed-form compiler, with no LLM in the loop, that grafts exact arithmetic and Boolean circuits into pretrained LM weights, a hand-derived counterpart to agent-written capabilities.
- [Can an LLM Repair a Language Model in Weight Space Without Gradient Training?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25613) - *Bharath Kumar Bolla et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-19, 0 citations.* A controlled study of LLM-guided transformer repair that compares the editor's choices against fixed projections, black-box search and LoRA to show when interactive selection actually matters.
- [Replicating and Extending Weight-Space Programming: Frontier LLMs Compile Programs into Weights and Read Input Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21449) - *Roksana Goworek et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 1 citation.* Independent replication of LLM weight-space programming; finds that written networks behave like compiled programs and that weight reading relies mostly on the input layer.
- [Just sort it! A note on the LP behind rank-one anti-loop edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21432) - *Matteo Fischetti, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 1 citation.* Shows that the sparse linear program behind the agent's rank-one anti-loop edit can be replaced by a simple sort, simplifying a celebrated WSP fix.
- [Same Fix, Different Weights: Replicating Weight-Space Programming with Claude](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20953) - *Subham Kundu, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-16, 1 citation.* Pre-registered replication of weight reading, repair, and writing with a different frontier model, with untrained-network and permutation controls.
- [Can Large Language Models Optimize Pretrained Neural Network Weights? A Multi-Phase Empirical Evaluation of Gradient-Free Editing in YOLO26 Object Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17789) - *Mohamed Abdallah Salem, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-13, 0 citations.* A negative result: on YOLO detectors, LLM-proposed weight edits show no significant advantage over a matched random-policy control or classical gradient-free optimizers.
- [Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210) - *Hadi Salloum and Ammar Ali, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-10, 1 citation.* Replaces an LLM agent's weight repair with a sparse discrete optimization over the model's own bits, a solver-based contrast to agent-driven editing.
- [Can an LLM Actually Reason and Program about Neural Network's Weights or is it just memorization on steroids? Exploring "Weight-Space Programming"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08070) - *Nazmus Sakib Ahmed, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-05, 7 citations.* Probes whether an LLM reading raw CNN weights is reasoning about the weights or relying on local arithmetic plus memorized dataset priors.
- [Computation, not weight access: large language models as auditors of clinical prediction networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07847) - *Zhiyu Liu and Xinyu Yang, arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-05, 4 citations.* Separates an LLM reading weights in its prompt from an LLM running code on them, and shows that reliable auditing of clinical networks comes from computation, not weight access.

### Security

Using weight-space programming to implant backdoors, and the risks this creates.

- [Autonomous Parameter Surgery: How LLMs Implant Selective Backdoors into Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27920) - *Xuyang Liu et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-20, 0 citations.* Shows that an LLM can turn a natural-language backdoor specification into persistent rank-one or single-neuron weight edits on another LM, a security risk of weight-space programming.
- [TrojanMaxxing: One-shotting VLM Backdoors with Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27425) - *Ahaan Dabholkar et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-20, 0 citations.* LLM agents inspect a VLM's weights, edit them, and co-design a visual trigger to implant a backdoor in one shot with no training.
- [Can LLMs Write Backdoors? An Empirical Security Study of Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17395) - *Zhaojia Wang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-12, 2 citations.* Security study of several LLMs acting as weight editors that inject backdoors into MLPs, ResNets, and a small LM with few parameter changes.

## Foundations and Related Areas

Older lines of work that treat weights as objects to compute, compose, learn from, or read, and that weight-space programming builds on.

### Model and Knowledge Editing

Methods that change specific facts or behaviours by computing or learning a targeted weight update.

- [MuEdit: An efficient multi-task editing method towards inter-domain knowledge conflicts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14910) - *Songshi Liang et al., NeurIPS 2026. Published 2026-11-11, 0 citations.* Explains multi-domain edit collapse as a shrinking shared null space and expands it with conflict-aware ordering and low-rank updates (MuEdit).
- [Capacity Allocation at the Source: Sparse Target Optimization for LLM Knowledge Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12995) - *Dongyao Chen et al., NeurIPS 2026. Published 2026-11-08, 0 citations.* Traces cross-edit interference in batched editing to overlapping target vectors and reduces it with sparse targets.
- [AnyEdit++: Adaptive Long-Form Knowledge Editing via Bayesian Surprise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01053) - *Bowen Tian et al., ICML 2026. Published 2026-05-31, 2 citations.* Long-form knowledge editing that segments targets by surprisal and uses orthogonal anchor keys to limit interference between weight updates (AnyEdit++).
- [From Backward Spreading to Forward Replay: Revisiting Target Construction in LLM Parameter Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00358) - *Wei Liu et al., ICML 2026. Published 2026-05-01, 4 citations.* Revisits how MEMIT spreads residual targets over layers and computes per-layer targets by forward replay instead.
- [CrispEdit: Low-Curvature Projections for Scalable Non-Destructive LLM Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15823) - *Zarif Ikram et al., ICML 2026. Published 2026-02-17, 7 citations.* Projects edits onto low-curvature directions of a capability-preserving loss, unifying null-space editors as a second-order method (CrispEdit).
- [MoEEdit: Efficient and Routing-Stable Knowledge Editing for Mixture-of-Experts LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10965) - *Yupu Gu et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-02-11, 5 citations.* A parameter-modifying editor for mixture-of-experts LLMs that keeps router inputs unchanged, so edits do not disturb routing (MoEEdit).
- [Steer2Edit: From Activation Steering to Component-Level Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09870) - *Chung-En Sun et al., NeurIPS 2026. Published 2026-02-10, 5 citations.* Converts activation-steering vectors into rank-one weight edits on selected heads and neurons, bridging steering and weight editing (Steer2Edit).
- [Multiplicative Orthogonal Sequential Editing for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07873) - *Hao-Xiang Xu et al., AAAI 2026. Published 2026-01-11, 2 citations.* Replaces additive weight deltas with multiplicative orthogonal updates that preserve the norm and conditioning of edited matrices.

### Analyses of Weight Edits

Work on what weight edits look like and how they fail, including rank-one structure, locality, side effects, and collapse under many edits.

- [On the Geometric Bottlenecks of Cross-Lingual Knowledge Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10646) - *Aastha A K Verma et al., NeurIPS 2026. Published 2026-11-07, 0 citations.* Decomposes in closed form why ROME, MEMIT, and AlphaEdit edits fail to carry across languages, pointing to representational anisotropy.
- [Selective Knowledge Edit Reversal via Gated Singular Vector Shrinkage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02091) - *Weifeng Jiang et al., Findings of EMNLP 2026. Published 2026-09-02, 1 citation.* Finds individual edits concentrated in a few singular components of the update, so they can be removed one at a time.
- [Exposing the Illusion of Erasure in Knowledge Editing for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23276) - *Advik Raj Basani and Anshuman Chhabra, NeurIPS 2026. Published 2026-06-22, 1 citation.* Shows that low-rank edits hide rather than erase old knowledge, which adversarial elicitation can recover.

- [Analyzing Transformers in Embedding Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02535) - *Guy Dar et al., ACL 2023. Published 2022-09-06, 164 citations.* Projects all transformer parameters into the embedding space so weights can be read directly without running inputs.
- [Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Build Predictions by Promoting Concepts in the Vocabulary Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14680) - *Mor Geva et al., EMNLP 2022. Published 2022-03-28, 688 citations.* Reads FFN value vectors by projecting them onto the vocabulary, and uses the reading to switch off weights that promote toxic tokens.
- [Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913) - *Mor Geva et al., EMNLP 2021. Published 2020-12-29, 1,885 citations.* Shows that FFN layers act as key-value memories whose weight rows can be read as input patterns and output distributions, the basis for MLP-targeted editing.

## Resources

Benchmarks, datasets, tools, model zoos, and surveys.

### Benchmarks, Datasets, Tools and Model Zoos

Evaluation suites for weight-space programming, weight edits and merges, software for manipulating weights, and collections of trained networks for weight-space research.

- [WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553) - *Aayush Marasini et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-13, 4 citations.* A benchmark of reading, repairing, and writing tasks that scores LLMs as weight-space programmers, from tiny policies up to 7B language models, including edits that should be refused as impossible.
- [IWIBench: Evaluating Gradient-Free Model Driven Intra-weight Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08647) - *Chris von Csefalvay et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-05, 3 citations.* Benchmarks an editor LLM that changes a target language model's behaviour only through read-weight and write-weight tools, with no gradients.
- [A Night at the LLM Zoo: Data Conditioned Flow Matching for Generating Pretrained LLM Weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07103) - *Scott Biggs et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-11-04, 0 citations.* Releases hundreds of GPT-2-scale models trained on controlled data mixtures and a data-conditioned flow-matching model of their weights.
- [BrainSurgery: Reproducible and Flexible Declarative Weight Manipulations for Model Editing and Upcycling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09707) - *Gianluca Barmina et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-06-08, 0 citations.* Tool that expresses checkpoint surgery (merging, low-rank rewriting, casting, upcycling) as declarative, reproducible plans over weight tensors.
- [Do Text Edits Generalize to Visual Generation? Benchmarking Cross-Modal Knowledge Editing in UMMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00477) - *Xin Gao et al., ICML 2026. Published 2026-05-30, 5 citations.* Benchmark showing that text-side weight edits in unified multimodal models rarely carry over to image generation (UniKE).
- [Are We Evaluating the Edit Locality of LLM Model Editing Properly?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17343) - *Wei Liu et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2026-01-24, 10 citations.* Shows that standard CounterFact and zsRE locality metrics are insensitive, and proposes a better protocol for measuring edit locality.
- [KnowledgeSmith: Uncovering Knowledge Updating in LLMs with Model Editing and Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02392) - *Yinyi Luo et al., ICLR 2026. Published 2025-10-01, 6 citations.* A unified framework and graph-structured benchmark for studying how weight edits and unlearning propagate (KnowledgeSmith).
- [MergeBench: A Benchmark for Merging Domain-Specialized LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10833) - *Yifei He et al., NeurIPS 2025 (Datasets and Benchmarks). Published 2025-05-16, 48 citations.* Benchmark for merging domain-specialized LLMs at realistic scale (MergeBench).
- [A Model Zoo of Vision Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10231) - *Damian Falk et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2025-04-14, 6 citations.* A model zoo of vision transformers for weight-space research on modern architectures.
- [We Should Chart an Atlas of All the World's Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10633) - *Eliahu Horwitz et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2025-03-13, 38 citations.* Argues for and begins building an atlas of public models linked by their weight-space lineage.
- [Implicit-Zoo: A Large-Scale Dataset of Neural Implicit Functions for 2D Images and 3D Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17438) - *Qi Ma et al., NeurIPS 2024. Published 2024-06-25, 13 citations.* A large dataset of neural implicit functions for 2D images and 3D scenes, which serve as weight-space training data (Implicit-Zoo).
- [FusionBench: A Unified Library and Comprehensive Benchmark for Deep Model Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03280) - *Anke Tang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2024-06-05, 14 citations.* A unified benchmark and library for comparing model fusion and merging methods (FusionBench).
- [Arcee's MergeKit: A Toolkit for Merging Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13257) - *Charles Goddard et al., EMNLP 2024 (Industry). Published 2024-03-20, 265 citations.* The standard open-source toolkit for merging LLM weights, implementing task arithmetic, TIES, DARE, SLERP, and layer stacking. [[Code](https://github.com/arcee-ai/mergekit)]
- [A Comprehensive Study of Knowledge Editing for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01286) - *Ningyu Zhang et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2024-01-02, 197 citations.* Introduces the KnowEdit benchmark and compares editing methods by where in the model they write knowledge. [[Code](https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit)]
- [EasyEdit: An Easy-to-use Knowledge Editing Framework for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07269) - *Peng Wang et al., ACL 2024 (Demo). Published 2023-08-14, 134 citations.* Open-source framework implementing most weight-editing methods behind one interface, widely used for editing experiments (EasyEdit). [[Code](https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit)]
- [Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976) - *Roi Cohen et al., TACL 2024. Published 2023-07-24, 371 citations.* Evaluates whether an edited fact's logical ripple effects are updated too (RippleEdits).
- [Detecting Edit Failures In Large Language Models: An Improved Specificity Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17553) - *Jason Hoelscher-Obermaier et al., Findings of ACL 2023. Published 2023-05-27, 86 citations.* Adds harder specificity tests to CounterFact (CounterFact+), exposing that ROME and MEMIT edits leak into unrelated prompts.
- [MQuAKE: Assessing Knowledge Editing in Language Models via Multi-Hop Questions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14795) - *Zexuan Zhong et al., EMNLP 2023. Published 2023-05-24, 426 citations.* A multi-hop question benchmark showing that weight-edited models often fail to propagate edited facts to their implications (MQuAKE). [[Code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/MQuAKE)]
- [Sparsified Model Zoo Twins: Investigating Populations of Sparsified Neural Network Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13718) - *Dominik Honegger et al., arXiv preprint. Published 2023-04-26, 5 citations.* Pairs dense model zoos with sparsified twins to study how pruning shows up in weight space.
- [Model Zoos: A Dataset of Diverse Populations of Neural Network Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14764) - *Konstantin Schürholt et al., NeurIPS 2022. Published 2022-09-29, 57 citations.* Releases structured populations of trained networks as a standard dataset for weight-space learning. [[Code](https://github.com/ModelZoos/ModelZooDataset)]
- [Classifying the classifier: dissecting the weight space of neural networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05688) - *Gabriel Eilertsen et al., ECAI 2020. Published 2020-02-13, 85 citations.* Builds a large set of trained classifiers and shows that properties such as training data and hyperparameters can be read from their weights.
